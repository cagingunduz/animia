# Collage Documentary — Style Prompt

> A documentary reel built like a hand-made collage: archival photographs, paper cut-outs with real shadows, a real
> portrait, big serif words that land exactly on the voice, hand-drawn white marks, little stop-motion movements.
> Follows the method in Naomi Metzger's (@naometzger) guide to documentary-style reels (look and rules only).

## 1. Essence (if it looks like AI, check these in order)
1. **Texture.** A paper ground under everything; photos printed on it; grain, dust, a slight gate weave.
2. **Cut-outs.** Things look PLACED in the scene, like a collage: each person / object is a separate piece with its own
   drop shadow (light from the upper left, shadow to the lower right), placed on a plate or on paper.
3. **Words.** Big serif italic words land exactly as the voice says them, in the scene (on the wall, in the sky, over
   the paper), never as a subtitle bar. One to four words per scene, sometimes the whole short line.
4. **Hand-drawn marks.** A white ring around one word or object pulls the eye straight to it; underlines, arrows, a
   cross-out.
5. **Movement.** Arms, hands and people move in small steps, like stop motion (the kit steps them at 12 fps). A slow
   push on every scene; nothing is ever still.

## 2. Scenes
- ONE clear idea per line (or per two lines). Scale and contrast tell the story: a tiny man under two giant clapping
  hands, a tiny piano under a giant ear trumpet, a full hall seen from the stage behind one figure.
- A scene = a ground (a photo plate full-bleed, or the paper / dark paper) + 1-3 pieces + the line's word(s) + maybe a mark.
- The real portrait appears ONCE, at the reveal or the payoff, in a frame, with a small caption (name and dates). Before
  that, the person is only seen from behind, far away or as a silhouette.
- Cuts land on the first word of the new line: a hard cut or a torn-paper cut (the torn sheet sweeps up). 1-3 torn cuts
  per reel, hard cuts elsewhere.
- Open on the strongest image within the first second. End on the payoff word or the portrait, then hold.

## 3. Colour and type
- Black and white / sepia archival photographs on warm off-white paper, or on dark board for night / sad moments. One
  palette for the whole reel; the portrait may keep its faded colour (it is the only colour, and it lands).
- Words: Playfair Display italic (display) 110-190 px for the big words; Cormorant italic for small captions. White on
  photos and dark paper (with a soft shadow), near-black ink on the light paper. Keep words off busy areas of a photo;
  never over a face.

## 4. Sound
- One calm British narrator. Music: melancholic felt piano and soft strings, ~15 dB under the voice. Foley: a paper
  rustle when a piece lands, pen scratch on each mark, a soft low tick when a big word lands, a rip on torn cuts, a knock
  when a frame hangs, a hit on the last line.

## 5. Mistakes to avoid
- A cartoon look: limbs that are not attached, flat clip-art, smooth floaty tweens. Use cut-outs with shadows and
  stepped movement.
- Music notes, icons or symbols drawn from fonts (they look "vibecoded"): use pictures or hand-drawn marks.
- Words parked as a subtitle bar, text over text, text over faces, a scene with nothing moving.
- Generated faces of real people. Facts that are not true.
