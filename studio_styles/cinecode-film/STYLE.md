CINECODE FILM: a long 16:9 YouTube film drawn entirely in code (no generated pictures, no photographs), in ONE look
from the cinecode library (its recipe follows below as THE LOOK RECIPE). The rules here come from cinecode's craft,
writing and motion-design references (github.com/sarpstar/cinecode, MIT), adapted to a narrated long video.

WHAT IS ON SCREEN
- The subject itself, built for the film, at every key beat: a machine as a 3D model or an engineering drawing, a city
  as blocks, a process as a diagram that builds, a number as a counter / bars / a grid of dots, a place as a map drawn
  in the look. Never a stand-in that could belong to any other film.
- No photographs exist in this template: where a recipe speaks of photos or scans, draw the thing (a document is a
  typeset card with its key line highlighted; a person is a stylised figure without a face, or their name on a card).
- A device earns its place when it shows what the voice cannot: a distance, the scale of a number, the inside of a
  thing, an order in time, a relation. Never decoration (floating shapes, particles, glow, a generic HUD, lens flares),
  never the same device until it is wallpaper.

SHOTS AND RHYTHM
- A new picture or a new step every 3-6 s while the voice speaks; each shot has ONE read and ONE action (something
  appears, builds, moves, turns, counts). Camera motion alone is not a shot: the camera moves with the action.
- A fresh framing at every cut (wide / medium / close, or a new angle); moves are already moving on the first frame of a
  shot and still moving at the cut, unless stillness is the point.
- Every cut has a reason you could say out loud: the next step of a process, this becomes that, into or out of the
  subject, this caused that, later, back to an earlier image changed.
- Tempo is a map: fast stretches (2-4 s steps), breaths (room for a line to land) and holds (a long look) alternate;
  never three of one in a row.
- Key moments (2-4 per part at most: the thesis, the hardest number, the turn): time on screen = reading time + 1.5 s,
  everything else steps back, no competing text.
- The hook: the first 2 seconds of the first part move from frame 1 with an action, never a title on an empty ground.

TEXT ON SCREEN
- At most 6 words on a card or a statement, on screen at least 0.6 s + 0.25 s per word; numbers from the script only.
- In the look's type roles (display / text / mono / hand), placed where the composition leaves room, inside a 5 % margin.
- Narration never gets burned in as subtitles. A label names one thing inside the picture; a statement carries the one
  line to remember.
- Never a dash in on-screen words (no em or en dash): a comma, a colon or a line break.

THE LOOK
- Follow the recipe's colour logic, materials, motion quality, camera grammar and native moves exactly (the recipe's
  engine section is replaced by this kit). One accent colour, spent the way the recipe says.
- Style serves the subject: heavy texture lives in the ground and secondary layers; the subject's silhouette stays
  readable at phone size.
