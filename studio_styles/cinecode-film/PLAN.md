THE FILM: narrated, 16:9, drawn entirely in code in one cinecode look. Nothing is generated or downloaded: every shot is
something an animator can BUILD (3D blocks, figures, machines, a city, a map; or 2D cards, diagrams, charts, counters,
labels, marker strokes, UI panels) in the look below.

WRITING (after cinecode's writing rules)
- The opening: 0-5 s a pattern break (a striking fact, a contradiction, a moment from later), 5-30 s why it matters and
  one hard fact, the story moving before 30 s. Within the first minute plant the question the ending answers; each part
  answers part of it and opens a smaller one; a reveal or reversal every 60-90 s.
- Narration never describes what is on screen: it adds what the picture cannot (a cause, a count, a contrast, the past).
- One idea per line, at most about 16 words, concrete nouns and verbs, no adjective stacks.
- Banned: "in a world where", "journey" as a metaphor, "little did they know", "what if", "more than just",
  "against all odds", "it's not about X, it's about Y", "the rest is history", "imagine", "let's", "here's the thing",
  "dive into", "unlock", "tapestry", "testament", "vibrant", "pivotal", "seamless", "breathtaking", rhetorical questions
  to the viewer, lists of three abstract nouns, ending on a slogan, and dashes (no em or en dash) in any line.
- Real people and events: only true claims, numbers never rounded into stronger ones; what is uncertain is said so.
- The ending ties back to the opening question (answers it, turns it, or leaves it open on purpose) and keeps a few
  seconds after the last line.

SHOTS (one every 3-6 s of narration, each on the word that names it)
- `build`: WHAT is built or drawn and what it does (a concrete thing in this story, never a generic stand-in: "a cutaway
  of the 1960s reactor core, rods sliding down", not "an energy graphic").
- `device`: the job it does: where / how far (a route on a map, a scale bar), how much (a counter, bars, a dot grid,
  two quantities side by side), how it works (an exploded view, a cutaway, a diagram that builds, labels on leaders),
  when (a timeline, a date that turns), who (a figure and a name card), statement (the one line to remember).
- `camera`: one camera line from the cinecode vocabulary: "dolly in, 3 s, slow ease-in-out, toward the core";
  push-in, pull-back, truck left/right, crane up, pan, tilt, orbit 30 deg, top-down, isometric drift, rack to.
- `text`: the words on screen (6 words at most), `data`: the real numbers a chart or counter uses.
- Shots in one part share a world where they can (the same city, the same machine), so the camera can travel through it.
