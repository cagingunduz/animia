THE FORMAT: a sci-fi sitcom cartoon reel. A DIALOGUE, not a narration: every line is spoken by one of the cast, and
`who` names the speaker. There is no narrator.

THE CAST (the same in every episode; never add another speaking character):
- VASK: a jaded genius in a bathrobe and goggles. Deadpan, short, dry sentences; sighs at everything; solves any
  problem with absurdly big sci-fi means (a portal remote marked DO NOT). Often starts his lines with "Gary."
- GARY: his nervous sidekick in a red helmet and big glasses. Fast, polite, stammering ("uh", "buh, buh-broken"),
  asks the questions the viewer has, panics, pays the emotional price.
- DECAF: a small talking coffee cup with eyes. Optional; at most two very short lines (a sweet or deadpan button).

THE ENGINE: a tiny, mundane goal (or a plain question about the topic) pursued with an absurdly large sci-fi means:
Vask opens a portal to a parallel world where the topic is taken to its extreme, and the joke is the gap between
their reactions. The topic must really be explained or shown by the end: each world demonstrates one true fact.

STRUCTURE (pick one):
- The question: Gary asks about the topic; Vask's one-line answer; "what if..."; a portal jump to the world where it
  is literally true; Gary's panic; Vask's deadpan one-line moral; a button (Decaf or a callback to line 1).
- Rule of three: two slow wrong worlds, then a fast third that works (or the suspiciously normal one).
- The errand: a petty chore that goes through 2-3 worlds, each one teaching a fact about the topic.

DIALOGUE RULES
- 8-16 lines, 70-150 words in all. Lines of 2-14 words; many very short ("Gary." / "No." / "Science.").
- Write for text-to-speech: spell stutters as syllables ("buh, buh-broken"), no dashes for interruptions, no stage
  directions in the text, no sound effects in the text. Numbers as spoken words in `say` when they differ.
- `mood` on every line: VASK is mostly deadpan (sometimes warm, rarely angry); GARY confused, excited or panic;
  DECAF warm or excited. A whisper for a secret, sad for the quiet button. The mood must match the words.
- `pause` after a line: 0.15-0.3 in quick back-and-forth, 0.6-0.9 before a punchline (the silence is the joke).
- Only true facts about the topic. Clean comedy: no swearing, no burping, nothing from any existing TV show.
- visual_notes: one line per voice line: the world (home lab or which new world, its palette and its creature), the
  shot (two-shot, close-up of whom, wide with the portal), what happens (portal opens on which word, who lands).
