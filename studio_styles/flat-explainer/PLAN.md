THE FORMAT: a long YouTube explainer (16:9) in the flat vector infographic-animation genre: a what-if, a "why does X
happen", a true story, or how something works. One lively narrator; every scene is a full illustrated place with
characters acting the line out; text only on signs, boards and screens in the world.

THE STRUCTURE (the 60-second arc, stretched over the whole video and repeated in each part):
- Part 1 (the opening, ~60 s): 0 s a second-person hook that flips what the viewer assumes, acted out literally, then a
  red X on the denial line -> the stakes in one shocking true line (props popping in, one per word) -> the subject
  revealed under a spotlight, its name typed on a sign.
- Middle parts (each 50-90 s, one per big question): the origin (the real place on its own sign, a date card, people
  walking in or out), the types or parts (landing one by one on their names), "so how does it work?" as a headline,
  the mechanism acted out in 2-3 places (a chalkboard, a machine, a map) with a counter rolling up to the big number,
  what it leads to (characters react: jump, turn, a "!").
- Last part: the twist that ties back to the hook; the last line is a question to the viewer, the viewer character
  from the hook reacts, and the sign holds to the end.
- Every part opens with a hook line of its own (a question or a surprising fact) so viewers keep watching.

RULES
- Length: about 3.1 words per second of the target length (e.g. 5 minutes = about 930 words), split into parts of
  150-280 words. Short spoken lines (4-16 words), plain English, contractions, true facts only (soften anything
  disputed); "you" speaks to the viewer.
- One recurring viewer character (the "you" of the hook) appears in every part.
- Few places, reused: 1-3 places per part (a place can host several shots with different framing and characters);
  4-10 characters in total, each with 1-3 poses they need (pointing, walking side view, seen from behind, sitting,
  shocked, thinking).
- Shots: one per line or per idea change (2-5 s each), each says: the place, who is in it and what they do, the text
  on a surface (and which), and any mark (red X, date card, headline, spotlight, counter, icons, tick).
